Chapter I  ·  How It Began

Two Strangers
in the Same Classroom

It started the way most things do — quietly, by accident. Class 11. The same tiled corridors, the same worn-out benches, the same nervous hum of teenagers figuring out who they were going to be.

He noticed her the way you notice a song drifting from a room you didn't expect to find music in. She laughed at something a friend said and he found himself listening to the laugh instead of the joke. He didn't know it yet — but she was already becoming everything.

🩷
Him
Her

They became friends — the kind built in late-night conversations. Family problems. Private fears. Secrets that only feel safe when given to someone who holds them carefully.

He was there for her every time she needed it. She was warm, grateful, present.
But they were not reading the same story.

Chapter III  ·  The Photograph

Farewell Night ✨

The Night of Fairy Lights

Class 12's farewell. Fairy lights across the ceiling. Everyone slightly overdressed. They stood together for a photo — just classmates, just a moment.

But he kept it. He studied it. Her smile. The light in her eyes.
He made it mean something it was never meant to mean.

"Love is the world's most honest feeling
— and its most talented fiction writer."

Chapter V  ·  What Was Said

The Messages That
Changed Everything

The Next Morning — She Texted First

She was kind about it. Direct and thoughtful. Her family was strict, her focus was her future. Love wasn't something she had space for.

"I don't have any interest in these things. You deserve better."
He Replied With Everything He Had

He didn't get angry. He didn't plead. He just told her the truth — the simplest, most devastating truth a person can tell.

"I don't want someone better. I don't want someone worse. I only want you."
Her Final Reply

She left him with words meant to be gentle — that landed like a door quietly closing.

"Let's just focus on studies for now."

And then the texts stopped. Not from cruelty. From clarity.

He Never Stopped Hoping

He kept the photograph. He told himself: one day she'll feel it too. He was wrong. But he wasn't broken. He was just completely, helplessly, in love.

Chapter VI  ·  The Day It Broke

His Birthday

The day goodness and a terrible mistake lived side by side

🍱
What He Did Right

On his birthday, he fed homeless children. Bought food with his own money. Sat with them. Made sure they had enough. Quiet, selfless — born from a heart trying to find meaning inside its own grief.

📸
What He Did Wrong

He shared the farewell photograph publicly. Without asking her. Without thinking about what it meant to her, her family, the private world she had carefully built. One impulsive decision. Everything collapsed.

💧
She Cried

When she found out, she cried. Not from hate — but because her privacy had been broken by someone she thought understood her.

"I will never forgive you. I never want to hear your name."
🌑
What Happened to Him

Those words were the heaviest thing he'd ever carried. That night, alone, he hurt himself. A blade. Blood. A boy drowning in guilt too large for his age. He survived. But something went very dark.

Chapter VII  ·  The Betrayal

By His Own Friends

They Were Supposed to Be His People

While she was crying, her friends came to comfort her. And in that room, they told her something that changed everything permanently.

They told her he would misuse the photograph. That he knew AI. That he could do anything with technology. That she should be afraid of what he was capable of.

These were not strangers. These were also his friends. People he had sat beside in the same corridors for years. People he had trusted.

In one moment, they took the very skill he had used to show her love — the website he built, the AI he had spent years learning — and turned it into a weapon against his character. In front of her. While she was already crying.

Rejection hurts. But betrayal by your own people breaks something deeper — something that takes much longer to heal.

The Spark That Died

He doesn't code anymore. Not because he forgot how — but because opening a laptop feels like opening a wound. The boy who used to see the world as something he could build and improve now sees it as something that punishes you for trying.

He doesn't trust people anymore. Not his classmates. Not his friends. Not even the parts of himself that used to believe in goodness. Every conversation feels like a performance. Every kindness feels like a trap waiting to spring.

The worst part isn't the pain.
It's the numbness that came after.
The feeling that nothing will ever matter again.

He didn't just lose the girl.
He didn't just lose the friends.
He deleted himself.

The boy who stayed up late teaching himself to code.
Who figured out AI when no one his age was even looking.
Who built a whole website for someone he loved —
because creation was his love language.

That boy erased three years of himself in one night.
And now he sits somewhere, scared of his own hands.
Unable to trust anyone. Unable to open a laptop
without hearing their words echo back.

Chapter VIII  ·  What He Needs to Say

A letter that was never sent
Class 11  ·  Never delivered
To her,
I know you've heard I've moved on.
I haven't. Not yet. And I'm not asking you to care about that. What I did — sharing our photograph without asking you — was wrong. Not wrong because I got in trouble. Wrong because it was your image, your privacy, your trust, and I broke all three in one thoughtless, lonely moment. And I know what your friends said. That I know AI. That I could misuse it. That I was dangerous. I understand why they said it. I understand why it made sense to you in that moment. But I want to tell you something true: I built things to create, never to harm. The website I made for you — two days before the farewell — I made it because building things is the only language I know to say "I see you, and I care." I deleted it all that night. Every project. Three years of work. Not because I was asked to. Because I couldn't stand the thought of you being afraid of something I made with love. I am sorry. I'm sorry for making you cry.
I'm sorry for sharing your image without asking.
I'm sorry that the thing I used to show you love became the thing that scared you.
I'm sorry for all of it — even the parts that weren't entirely my fault. I only want five minutes. Face to face. To say this out loud, so I can stop carrying it everywhere I go. After that, I will leave. Quietly. For good.
And I will rebuild — slower this time, and only for myself.
Him
The boy who built worlds and forgot to protect yours

He was just a boy who loved someone
with his whole chest —
who built worlds from lines of code
because it was the only way he knew
to say  "I see you."
They took his greatest gift
and made her afraid of it.
And he — in the depths of his grief —
deleted three years of himself
just to prove he was safe.
"No mistake, however terrible,
can delete who you truly are.
Only you can do that.
And only you can rebuild."
What he needs to know now
1
Her "no" was not a verdict on his worth. It was honesty — the kindest thing she could have given him.
2
What those friends said was cruel and untrue. His skills were never a weapon. They were always a love language. Always.
3
Deleting three years does not make him safer or better. It only makes him smaller. The work was never the problem — and it deserves to be rebuilt.
4
A blade and a cigarette will never carry this weight. He needs a real person — a counsellor, a trusted adult — someone who can actually sit with him in it.
5
One day he will open a laptop again. He will build something extraordinary — not for anyone else, just for the joy of making. That day is coming.
This story is not over.
He deleted three years.
He still has a whole life left to fill with code, with love,
with things only he can make.
